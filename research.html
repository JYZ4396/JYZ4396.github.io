Disaster debris and cleanup records

Independent research

Beyond construction-debris counts→

I studied five hurricanes to see which coastal communities appeared in volunteer cleanup records and where missing reports limited comparisons.

Statistical modeling

Predicting unusual debris in coastal cleanups→

A prediction model for unusual coastal debris relied heavily on reporting year. I tested how its performance changed when I removed that variable.

Conference poster

Unusual Coastal Trash: The Danger and Solution→

After helping clear debris in Cedar Key, I examined ten years of Florida cleanup records to see what volunteers reported beyond familiar plastic items.

Florida community research

Collaborative research

Coastal infrastructure priorities in Cedar Key→

I helped check the team’s models and interpret the survey results. In the manuscript’s methods section, I explained why residents’ stakeholder nominations and their rankings of local places called for different models.

Community research

Housing and environmental health in Keystone Heights→

I surveyed residents and checked their original Qualtrics responses against the project dashboard. For several questions, I traced incorrect answer labels back to a five-point shift in the exported values.

Community research

Housing and respiratory health in Jacksonville→

I collected resident surveys in Jacksonville’s Health Zone 1 and organized housing information for 60 participants.

Research collaborations

Collaborative research

Learning and assessment in disaster recovery training→

I reviewed disaster scenarios for five learner personas, checking whether someone in each role would face the decisions described. Our team also explored using an AI agent to simulate how each persona might respond.
