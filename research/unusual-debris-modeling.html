Predicting unusual debris in coastal cleanups

In a Python modeling course, I used a Markov chain to predict changes in the dominant category of unusual debris. Its limited predictive performance led me to try a related question at the level of an individual cleanup.

My role
Python course project and independent follow-up
ML project period
June–August 2026
Setting
U.S. coastal cleanup records (ML analysis)

What did the classifier learn?

I used 131,909 U.S. cleanup records to identify events in the top quarter by share of unusual items. I compared logistic regression, random forest, and gradient boosting on a stratified test split. Gradient boosting had the highest average precision (0.638).

When I removed the reporting year and retrained the model, average precision fell to 0.509. Since the categories volunteers could report changed over time, I repeated the analysis on 2023–2025 records, when the categories were more consistent. In the 2023–2025 model, cleanup zone, longitude, and latitude ranked highest in permutation importance, while reporting year ranked fifth.
