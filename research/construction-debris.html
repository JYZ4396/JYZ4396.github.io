Beyond construction-debris counts

I linked Ocean Conservancy cleanup records to hurricane wind exposure and county housing data in Florida, Georgia, North Carolina, and South Carolina. For five hurricanes from 2022 to 2024, I compared the 30 days before and after each storm and tracked where records were available.

How did reported debris counts change across the five hurricanes?

I used a weighted Poisson model to compare each county before and after the same hurricane. The model estimated 35% more reported construction-debris items per cleanup after a storm, but the interval was wide enough to include both a decrease and an increase (incidence rate ratio 1.35, 95% CI 0.82–2.22).

Which communities had usable cleanup records?

Usable records from both periods were available for 4 of 53 county–hurricane pairs (7.5%) in the high housing-vulnerability group and 40 of 124 (32.3%) in the low group. All four high-vulnerability pairs came from one county, Colleton County, South Carolina.

How did the results vary by hurricane?

Idalia showed the clearest increase, an estimated 4.29 more reported construction-debris items per cleanup after the storm (95% CI 2.53–6.04). That estimate was substantially smaller when I excluded particular counties or cleanup dates. For each of the other four hurricanes, the confidence interval included zero.
