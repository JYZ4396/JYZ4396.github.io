Housing and environmental health in Keystone Heights

Our lab worked with a community center in Keystone Heights, Florida, to understand residents’ experiences of housing, infrastructure, and environmental health.

Checking survey responses

After field visits, I wrote code to flag incomplete questionnaires and responses that repeated the same rating across a series of questions. The flags identified records for closer review.

Auditing the dashboard

When we exported our survey data from Qualtrics to the dashboard, the system flagged cases where a housing location did not match the corresponding survey record. I checked the location information in the original records against what appeared on the dashboard to see whether the mismatch was already present in our dataset or whether it arose when the dashboard processed the records.

I also ran a separate check to make sure the survey responses in our dataset appeared correctly on the dashboard after export. Then I found that the dashboard was displaying the wrong labels for responses to several questions. For example, on the housing affordability question, a response of 1 meant “extremely unaffordable.” The dashboard showed that same response as “extremely affordable,” which corresponded to 6 on the original scale. To understand why, I compared the response codes in Qualtrics with the values in the exported file. For the affected questions, the exported values were five points higher than the original codes. Once I traced the incorrect labels back to that five-point shift, we were able to correct how the dashboard displayed the affected responses.
